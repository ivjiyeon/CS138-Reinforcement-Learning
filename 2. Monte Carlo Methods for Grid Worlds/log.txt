User: can you explain again why off policy method in part 1 falls off largely compared to on policy methods?

Agent: The leading explanation is how off-policy MC assigns credit. I haven't run an ablation to confirm it, so treat it as the likely cause rather than a proven one.

**How off-policy MC uses an episode.** The algorithm walks backwards from the end of the episode and stops at the first action that the greedy target policy wouldn't have taken. Everything before that point, which is most of the episode, gets zero importance weight and teaches nothing. So each episode only updates the tail where the behaviour policy happened to agree with the greedy policy at every step.

**Why that tail is short in the racetrack.** Under an $\varepsilon$-greedy behaviour policy with $\varepsilon=0.1$, the chance of a non-greedy action on any step is roughly 0.09. The chance that the last $k$ steps are all greedy is therefore about $0.91^k$:

| tail length $k$ | chance all greedy |
|---|---|
| 10 | ~0.39 |
| 30 | ~0.06 |
| 100 | ~0.0001 |

Early in training the policy is bad, so episodes run to the 300-step cap, and the usable tail is typically only a handful of steps near the end. States near the start line are almost never updated. To learn them, the agent needs a whole episode of greedy actions leading to the finish, and that is exactly what it can't do yet.

**Why on-policy MC doesn't have this problem.** Its first-visit update applies to every state-action pair visited in the episode, whatever action was taken, because the behaviour policy and the policy being improved are the same. Each episode gives credit to all of its states, so the long, exploratory early episodes still teach something useful.

**Why it works in the Part 3 grid world.** Episodes there are short (tens of steps) and the state space is tiny, so the greedy tail is long relative to the episode and covers most of the states. The same algorithm learned well there, which fits the explanation above. It's consistent with the mechanism but doesn't prove it.

**Two things that likely make it worse in Part 1:**
- **Optimistic initialisation:** $Q=0$ makes the greedy policy keep changing as states get tried and drop below zero. A moving target means the "all greedy" tail from one episode may no longer match the greedy policy by the time it's used.
- **No tuning:** I used the default behaviour $\varepsilon=0.1$. A smaller value would lengthen the usable tails, so the off-policy result reflects default settings, not the best this method can do.

If you want to check the explanation, there are two cheap experiments. You could log how many steps each off-policy episode actually updates, to see whether the average really is a few steps. Or you could rerun off-policy with behaviour $\varepsilon=0.02$ and see whether learning speeds up.
